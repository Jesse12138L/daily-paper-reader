Instruction-Guided Navigation and Dynamic Person Tracking
Pengfei Qi1, Haoran Lin2, Sizhuang Chen2, Kai Luo2, Sirui Zhang2, Xinqi Liu2, Fei Cheng3,4,
Wenrui Chen2, Liming Yin4, and Kailun Yang1,2,†

Abstract— General-purpose embodied robots should support
both navigation toward language-specified destinations and
dynamic person tracking under arbitrary initial target az-
imuths. However, existing methods typically rely on forward-
facing observations and address these tasks with separate
policies, limiting omnidirectional perception and unified closed-
loop control. We present UniTrackPLA, a unified panorama-
language-action model for instruction-guided navigation and
dynamic person tracking. Its Panoramic-Aware Encoding (PAE)
preserves the temporal and azimuthal structure of perspective
views projected from each panorama, enabling perspective-
pretrained visual encoders to process omnidirectional observa-
tions. A shared vision-language backbone grounds instructions
in the panoramic context and predicts continuous robot-centric
waypoint chunks for both tasks. World-Action Consistency
(WAC) further predicts action-conditioned future visual states
and verifies waypoint prefixes online, allowing reliable actions
to be reused while triggering replanning upon inconsistency.
We also introduce OmniTrackNav-Bench, comprising 5, 000
simulated tracking trajectories, 10, 000 simulated VLN routes,
and 96 verified real-world routes, providing 919, 978 waypoint-
supervision instances. UniTrackPLA improves overall track-
ing SR from 23.50% to 35.00% and Omni-VLN SR/SPL
from 13.00%/12.77% to 19.75%/19.29%. Incorporating 76
real-world routes further improves held-out EP@0.2m from
42.92% to 92.08%. Closed-loop experiments on a Go2-W
robot demonstrate unified panoramic tracking and navigation
across indoor and outdoor environments. The project page is
at https://tw5775.github.io/UniTrackPLA.

I. INTRODUCTION
Embodied navigation enables robots to move through
an environment according to visual observations and task
instructions, constituting a fundamental capability for house-
hold assistance, human accompaniment, inspection, and mo-
bile manipulation [1], [2], [3], [4]. Existing studies, how-
ever, have largely evolved along two separate paradigms.
Vision-language navigation mainly focuses on reaching static
semantic goals specified by language instructions [5], [6],
[7], [8], whereas embodied visual tracking emphasizes con-
tinuously following dynamic targets whose positions evolve
over time [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]. Recent Vision-Language-
Action (VLA) models integrate visual perception, language
understanding, and action prediction, improving instruction
following and generalization in unseen environments [2], [5],
[9], [14]. Nevertheless, static-goal navigation and dynamic
person tracking are still commonly treated separately, with
task-specific observations, action spaces, and policies.
We argue that these two tasks should instead be viewed as
complementary instances of a broader embodied navigation
problem. Instruction-guided navigation is primarily driven
by relatively static semantic goals, while person tracking
requires continuous adaptation to dynamically evolving phys-
ical targets. Despite this difference, both require the robot
to ground task-relevant entities, reason about their spatial
relationships, and continuously translate visual-language ob-
servations into motion decisions. A more general embod-
ied navigation system should therefore be able to bridge
these two regimes within a shared perception-language-
action paradigm. This raises a fundamental question: can
a single policy support both instruction-guided navigation
toward static goals and continuous tracking of dynamic
persons under a unified observation and action interface?
Bridging static navigation and dynamic tracking requires a
shared omnidirectional perceptual representation. Panoramic
perception provides a natural means of removing this head-
ing dependence by exposing the surrounding scene within

a single omnidirectional observation space [2], [5], [15].
Prior studies have explored panoramic RGB-D observa-
tions [16], [17], omnidirectional images [18], multiple per-
spective views [7], panoramic environment generation [19],
[20], and latent panorama prediction [8] for static-goal
search and vision-language navigation. Recent approaches
further incorporate panoramic scene parsing [21], dynamic
memory [22], and global-to-local reasoning [23], [24]. Nev-
ertheless, existing panoramic systems mainly focus on static
object search, route following, and scene reasoning, while
dynamic target localization across arbitrary orientations and
panorama boundaries remains insufficiently explored. More
importantly, panoramic observations introduce a representa-
tion gap: most pretrained visual encoders are optimized for
perspective images, whereas panoramic perception requires
explicit reasoning over circular spatial structure and temporal
evolution. Therefore, the key challenge is not merely to
enlarge the field of view, but to retain the transferable rep-
resentations of pretrained perspective-image encoders while
recovering the spatial and temporal structure required for
unified panoramic embodied navigation.
A unified policy must also handle the different temporal
dynamics of static navigation and dynamic tracking. Con-
tinuous waypoint heads improve efficiency by predicting
short-horizon action chunks [5], [9], but fixed-horizon ex-
ecution may become unreliable as the visual world evolves:
trajectories that remain valid in relatively stable navigation
can quickly become stale when a tracked person changes
direction, local traversability changes, or execution errors ac-
cumulate. Executing the full chunk risks propagating invalid
actions, while using only the first waypoint sacrifices most
of the predicted trajectory. The key challenge is therefore
how long a predicted trajectory should remain trusted. Since
pixel-level world models are costly for real-time control [25],
[26], we instead employ lightweight action-conditioned latent
future prediction to adaptively regulate the execution horizon.
To bridge static instruction-guided navigation and dy-
namic person tracking across both perception and exe-
cution, we propose UniTrackPLA, a unified Panorama-
Language-Action framework. UniTrackPLA formulates both
tasks under shared panoramic observations and a continuous
waypoint action space. Panoramic-Aware Encoding (PAE)
captures visual content, view azimuth, and temporal position
while preserving compatibility with pretrained perspective-
image encoders, and a shared multimodal policy predicts
waypoint chunks for both navigation and tracking. We in-
troduce World-Action Consistency (WAC), which predicts
action-conditioned future states in latent space and adaptively
determines whether to continue the current chunk or replan,
enabling efficient yet responsive closed-loop control.
To support unified training and evaluation, we construct
OmniTrackNav-Bench, comprising Omni-Tracking with
5, 000 dynamic-following trajectories and Omni-VLN with
10, 000 language-annotated panoramic navigation routes.
Both tasks share the same panoramic observation and con-
tinuous waypoint representation, enabling unified multi-task
learning within a single policy. We introduce a real-world
panoramic navigation dataset comprising 96 routes.

• We propose UniTrackPLA, a panoramic VLA frame-
work that bridges dynamic person following and vision-

tion, visual-language representations, and a continuous
waypoint action space. Its PAE module jointly models
panoramic azimuth and temporal context while reusing
pretrained perspective-image encoders.
• We introduce a lightweight World-Action Consistency

static navigation and dynamic tracking. By predicting
the latent future consequences of waypoint prefixes,

the overall framework.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Embodied Navigation
Existing embodied navigation methods can be broadly di-
vided into static-goal navigation and dynamic-person follow-
ing. Static-goal navigation guides a robot toward a specified
location according to language instructions, target categories,
or reference routes. Recent methods unify multiple naviga-
tion tasks and improve generalization across environments,
embodiments, and long-horizon routes [2], [5], [14], while
observation-instruction augmentation further facilitates trans-
fer to unseen environments [27].
Dynamic person following instead requires continuous
target identification and motion adaptation. Hierarchical ap-
proaches bridge high-level user instructions and low-level
tracking actions through intermediate spatial goals [28].
Other modular approaches employ visual foundation models,
offline reinforcement learning, and vision-language models
to improve target representation, failure recovery, occlu-
sion handling, and cross-embodiment adaptation [10], [11],
[29], [30]. End-to-end methods further incorporate referring

B. Panoramic-Aware Embodied Navigation
Panoramic-aware navigation methods can be broadly
divided into panoramic-perception-based navigation and
panorama-assisted navigation. The former directly uses
panoramic RGB-D observations, omnidirectional images,
or multiple perspective views for target-driven, open-
vocabulary, and language-guided navigation [16], [17], [18].
In particular, view-decomposed panoramic representations
preserve local visual details while retaining compatibility
with visual encoders pretrained on perspective images. The
latter leverages generated panoramic environments, com-
plementary views, or predicted latent panoramic features
to bridge the observation gap between monocular and
panoramic navigation [7], [8], [19], [20]. Recent methods
further combine panoramic scene parsing, dynamic memory,
panorama-to-local reasoning, and 3D scene representations
for mapless object navigation and zero-shot vision-language
navigation [21], [22], [23], while other studies investigate
spherical directional reasoning and establish benchmarks for
panoramic urban navigation and general embodied navi-
gation [6], [24], [35]. Beyond navigation, omnidirectional
perception has also enabled beyond-FoV humanoid ma-
nipulation and panorama-aware mobile manipulation [4],
[36]. Nevertheless, existing panoramic embodied systems
primarily focus on static object search, route following, scene
reasoning, or manipulation, leaving dynamic person follow-
ing under arbitrary initial orientations, particularly cross-
view localization and identity preservation, less explored.
Moreover, navigation and person following are commonly
modeled using separate representations, action spaces, and
policies. A unified panoramic vision-language-action policy
for both instruction-guided navigation and dynamic person
following remains insufficiently studied.
III. METHOD
UniTrackPLA Overview. In this section, we put for-
ward the Unified Panorama-Language-Action (UniTrack-
PLA) framework to unify language-guided navigation and
dynamic-person following by leveraging panoramic obser-
vations within a single embodied policy. An overview of the
proposed UniTrackPLA model is shown in Fig. 2. First, the
Panoramic-Aware Encoding (PAE) module (see Sec. III-
A) projects each panorama into cyclic perspective views and
preserves their temporal and azimuthal identities, adapting
perspective-pretrained visual representations to panoramic
observations. Next, a shared vision-language policy (see
Sec. III-B) grounds task instructions in the panoramic context
and predicts a chunk of robot-centric waypoints for both
following and navigation. Finally, the World-Action Con-
sistency (WAC) module (see Sec. III-C) predicts the future
visual consequences of waypoint prefixes and verifies their
consistency during execution, enabling the controller to reuse
reliable actions or trigger replanning when the environment
deviates from the predicted transition.


A. Panoramic-Aware Encoding
Visual encoders pretrained on perspective images are
suboptimal for panoramic observations. Directly encoding
an equirectangular projection (ERP) causes non-uniform
distortion, while independently encoding perspective crops
loses their spatial arrangement, hindering cross-view target
association and directional grounding.
We introduce Panoramic-Aware Encoding (PAE) to pre-
serve the azimuthal and temporal identities of panoramic
features. Each ERP is projected into N cyclically ordered
perspective views with a configurable field of view. Frozen
DINOv3 [37] and SigLIP [38] encoders extract complemen-
tary features, which are concatenated and mapped into the
language-model embedding space by a trainable projector.

B. Unified Vision-Language-Action Policy
Language identifies a target by appearance in person fol-
lowing, while describing routes, landmarks, and destinations
in VLN. Despite these different semantics, both tasks require
continuous local motion planning from panoramic obser-
vations. We therefore use a unified vision-language-action
policy that shares its panoramic representation, multimodal
backbone, and action space, with task behavior determined
by the instruction and visual context.
The panoramic tokens from PAE and the instruction tokens
are jointly processed by a frozen Qwen3-0.6B backbone [39].
The language backbone is frozen to preserve its pretrained
semantics, while the multimodal projector, PAE embeddings,
and continuous action head remain trainable.

gressively, the action head directly predicts a waypoint chunk

C. World-Action Consistency
Waypoint chunking provides smooth short-horizon plans,
but behavior cloning does not model the future visual states
induced by predicted actions. Consequently, later waypoints
may become invalid under target motion, environmental
changes, or execution errors. We introduce World-Action
Consistency (WAC) to predict compact future visual features
conditioned on the current state and waypoint prefixes,
without reconstructing RGB observations.

mean-pooled and ℓ2-normalized into a visual state zt. Each
waypoint is projected into an action token, and a future query
is assigned to each prediction horizon. An action-conditioned

During training, WAC is conditioned on expert waypoint

and longest-horizon states, encouraging the predicted tran-

latent space, avoiding pixel-level generation.

how much of each waypoint chunk remains valid.

IV. EXPERIMENT SETUP
A. Dataset
Existing embodied navigation benchmarks commonly as-
sume a limited forward-facing field of view and treat person
following and vision-language navigation as separate tasks.
We introduce OmniTrackNav-Bench, a unified benchmark
for both tasks under omnidirectional perception. It comprises
Omni-Tracking, Omni-VLN, and a Real-World Dataset,
containing 5, 000 simulated person-following trajectories,
10, 000 simulated VLN routes, and 96 verified real-world
routes. Together, they provide 919, 978 waypoint-supervision
instances using 360◦ERP observations and a shared contin-
uous waypoint space.
Omni-Tracking. As shown in Fig. 3, Omni-Tracking con-

Each episode initializes the referred person at an arbitrary
azimuth, allowing the target to appear in front of, beside,
behind, or across the ERP seam. STF evaluates target lo-

multiple people and requires language-based grounding and

Unlike forward-view tracking datasets that generate tra-
jectories from image-space cues such as bounding-box size
and pixel count, these cues are unstable in ERP panoramas
because target appearance varies with azimuth, distance,
and seam location. We therefore generate trajectories from
the simulator-provided robot–target relative position, dis-
tance, and heading, subject to explicit following-distance
constraints. Omni-Tracking further contains a validation split
of 60 episodes, including 30 STF and 30 DRF episodes, and
an evaluation split of 200 episodes, including 100 per setting.
The training, validation, and evaluation splits have no episode
or source-route overlap.
Omni-VLN. Omni-VLN is constructed from R2R-CE and
RxR-CE, each contributing 5, 000 training routes. Since the
original routes provide observations only at sparse naviga-
tion nodes, we densely resample the executable paths and
render ERP observations at the sampled poses, producing
continuous panoramic navigation sequences.
At each decision point, a local future trajectory is rep-
resented as a fixed-length waypoint chunk. Each waypoint
specifies cumulative planar displacement and yaw change in
the current robot frame. The R2R-CE and RxR-CE subsets
contain 196, 136 and 288, 281 supervision instances, respec-
tively, totaling 484, 417 navigation decisions. The evaluation
split contains 400 routes, evenly distributed across R2R-CE
Seen, R2R-CE Unseen, RxR-CE Seen, and RxR-CE Unseen.
Real-World Dataset. We collect long-range trajectories us-
ing a handheld panoramic camera in diverse indoor and
outdoor environments, including roads, intersections, pedes-
trian paths, plazas, parking areas, building entrances, lakeside
walkways, bridges, commercial streets, shopping malls, su-
permarkets, and offices. The data cover indoor-outdoor tran-
sitions and both daytime and nighttime conditions. Each 360◦
ERP video is synchronized with its metric pose trajectory.

B. Implementation Details
All models are trained for one epoch on eight NVIDIA
RTX 3090 GPUs. DINOv3, SigLIP, and Qwen3-0.6B are
frozen, while the multimodal projector, PAE, action head,
and WAC are optimized. Based on the validation results,
which will unfold in ablation analyses, each panorama is
projected into four overlapping views at a resolution of
384 × 384, each with a horizontal field of view of 180◦.

and inverse-action losses are 10, 0.1, and 0.05, respectively.
All hyperparameters are selected on the validation sets and
fixed for final evaluation.
Evaluation Metrics. SR measures success; FR, LR, and CR
denote following, target-loss, and collision rates. SPL reflects
path efficiency. ADE, FDE, and Yaw MAE measure average,
final, and yaw errors, while EP@0.2m measures endpoint
accuracy within 0.2 m. Arrows indicate preference.
C. Quantitative Results
Human Tracking Results. Table I shows that UniTrack-

overall SR and FR by 11.5 and 6.4 percentage points,
respectively, while reducing loss and collision rates. In the
STF (top) and DRF (bottom) examples of Fig. 4, TrackVLA
approaches the targets aggressively and terminates after only
1.9s and 1.6s, whereas UniTrackPLA maintains safer, stable
following for 6.6s and 6.4s across scene transitions.
Vision-Language Navigation Results. Table II shows that
UniTrackPLA consistently outperforms TrackVLA across all
splits, with the largest gains in unseen environments. Overall,
it improves SR from 13.00% to 19.75% and SPL from
12.77% to 19.29%, demonstrating better navigation success
without sacrificing path efficiency.

D. Ablation Studies
We evaluate the panoramic view configuration and the
individual contributions of PAE and WAC.
1) Panoramic View Configuration: Table IV shows that
four 180◦views provide the best balance, achieving the
highest SR and FR and the lowest LR. Compared with a
single 360◦view, this configuration improves SR by 8.34
percentage points and reduces LR by 10.00 points; we
therefore adopt it by default. Narrower FoVs provide insuffi-
cient overlap, whereas additional views introduce redundant
information.
2) Effectiveness of PAE and WAC: Table V shows that
PAE alone improves overall SR from 23.50% to 32.50%.
WAC alone raises overall SR to 28.50% but reduces DRF SR
from 12.00% to 10.00%, indicating that consistency verifica-
tion benefits from explicit panoramic directional modeling.
Combining PAE and WAC achieves the best overall perfor-
mance, confirming their complementary roles in panoramic
representation and closed-loop execution verification.

V. CONCLUSION
We
presented
UniTrackPLA,
a
unified
panorama-
language-action model for instruction-guided navigation and
dynamic person tracking. PAE preserves temporal and
azimuthal structure across panoramic views, while WAC
models action-conditioned future visual states to support
consistency-guided waypoint reuse and replanning. We also
introduced OmniTrackNav-Bench, which unifies simulated
tracking, panoramic navigation, and real-world routes un-
der a shared observation and action space. Experiments
demonstrate consistent improvements over TrackVLA, while
real-world deployment confirms the benefits of panoramic
perception and real-data training. Future work will explore
uncertainty-aware replanning, broader embodied tasks, and
efficient fully onboard deployment.
